Thoughts (SN 56.7)

Saṃyutta Nikāya
Mahāvaggo
Saccasaṃyuttaṃ
Samādhivaggo
Vitakkasuttaṃ

Do not, almsmen, have thoughts as evil unwholesome thoughts; that is as this: lustful thoughts, angry thoughts, harmful thoughts.

Of what is this the reason?

Not these, almsmen, are thoughts put together for settlement of the beginning of the clergy conduct, they do lead not to disenchantment, not to dispassion, not to cessation, not to allaying, not to getting acquainted with, not to enlightenment, not to peace.

OK and you, almsman, when thinking, you should have the thought 'This, the suffering', you should have the thought 'This, the rise of suffering', you should have the thought 'This, the cessation of suffering', you should have the thought 'This, the path heading for the cessation of suffering'.

Of what is this the reason?

These, almsmen, are thoughts put together for settlement of the beginning of the clergy conduct, they do lead to disenchantment, to dispassion, to cessation, to allaying, to getting acquainted with to enlightenment, to peace.

Therefore, almsmen, with 'This, the suffering' is the yoke to be handled, with 'This, the rise of suffering' is the yoke to be handled, with 'This, the cessation of suffering' is the yoke to be handled, with 'This, the path heading for the cessation of suffering' is the yoke to be handled.'
