Mahābhūtā

Suppose we are looking at a red wooden chair. Here is red something we bring in as light just gets absorbed and reflected, which we here perceive as red. Despite this behaviour, of absorbing and reflecting, is what we perceive the way we express it (AN 6.63). Wooden chair is also something which we bring in as wood has no notion of being intended to sit on; it just behaves as wood does. And as it is our experience, it is personal. It may not feel that way as perceptions are similar between species, and we can communicate about them which gives the impression of them being public. But it are just the aspects (behaviour) of form that are public and which found footing in our experience when picked up; which then became this "coloured form". Both, and the behaviour of form and the form picked up are called form (SN 12.2). Form consists of a mix of four primary behaviours, the great essences (mahābhūtā). To have a view on them we could look for the more basic forms where such essences sticks out.

The great essences are:

Earth (pathavī). The behaviour of earth is that it occupies space. Like the planet, which is largely covered with water (udaka).

Fluidity (āpo). The behaviour of fluidity we can see with water as ice, liquid water, vapour. And thus also accounts for certain surfaces which have a role in absorbing and reflecting light.

Heat (tejo). The behaviour of heat is that it allows for change. AN 5.197 shows that this aspect can disturb upper in the air (ākāsa) by which arisen clouds go away. So the weather changes, temperature change (the change is felt as warm, hot), ice can melt, liquids can vaporize, it can deform.

Wind (vāyo). The behaviour of wind is the blowing; movement. Winds are blowing in and out, like with the in and out breathe or the winds in the belly.

The primary behaviours we thus see in earth, fluidity, heat and wind as each carries its distinguishing aspects more prominent. These aspects can be inner and outer. The body is of the four essences and when cast away temperature leaves the body, breathing stopped, winds come out, body fluids come out, and lastly the bones remain for a while till they are gone too. Here are the inner aspects represented. The outer aspects we can see represented for example through their excesses as calamities: earth in earthquakes, fluidity in floods, heat in heat waves and wind in hurricanes.

The point is that these essences follow their own agenda, no matter how we would like them to be, no matter how we perceive them to be. All the changes we can make to them are already granted by them; it is already within their domain. The internal earth aspect and the external earth aspect are just the earth aspect. Earth is earth through and through, idem for the others. And as their behaviour is always in accordance to their own domain they should not be seen as me, mine, myself, etc. But while the body isn't ours, the actions (kamma) with it are (SN 3.20).

Pāḷi-English

Mahābhūta great essence.
Pathavī (f.) earth.
Āpo (nt.) fluidity.
Tejo (nt.) heat.
Vāyo (nt.) wind.
