Calm plus Understanding (SN 43.2)

Saṃyutta Nikāya
Saḷāyatanavaggo
Asaṅkhatasaṃyuttaṃ
Paṭhamavaggo
Samathavipassanāsuttaṃ

'I shall point out to you, almsmen, and the uninclined and the way heading for the uninclined. Hear that.

And which, almsmen, the uninclined?

What, almsmen, is barrenness of lust, barrenness of hate, barrenness of delusion, this is called, almsmen, uninclined.

And which, almsmen, way heading for the uninclined?'

And calm and understanding, this is called, almsmen, the way heading for the uninclined.

OK thus, almsmen, I have pointed out to you the uninclined, pointed out the way heading for the uninclined. What, almsmen, should be done by a predecessor for the welfare of hearers, taking it up with mercy, out of mercy, that is done by me for you. These are, almsmen, roots of trees, these are empty homes; radiate, almsmen, don't be negligent, don't become regretful afterwards. This is our instruction to you.'
