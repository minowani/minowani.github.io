Characteristics of not Self (SN 22.59)

Saṃyutta Nikāya
Khandhavaggo
Upayavaggo
Anattalakkhaṇasuttaṃ

On one occasion the exalted one dwells at the deer park at Isipatana, at Bārāṇasi. OK there the exalted one addressed the almsmen of the group of five:

'Almsmen'

Those almsmen replied to the exalted one:

'Venerable sir'

The exalted one said this:

'Form, almsmen, is not self. And form, almsmen, for this self, if it becomes, this not would lead form to oppression and you would get at form: 'My form be so, as my form was not so'. OK and from what, almsmen, form is not self, from that form leads to oppression and you not get at form: 'My form be so, as my form was not so'.

Feeling, almsmen, is not self. And feeling, almsmen, for this self, if it becomes, this not would lead feeling to oppression and you would get at feeling: 'My feeling be so, as my feeling was not so'.

Perception, almsmen, is not self. And perception, almsmen, for this self, if it becomes, this not would lead perception to oppression and you would get at perception: 'My perception be so, as my perception was not so'.

Inclinations, almsmen, is not self. And inclination, almsmen, for this self, if it becomes, this not would lead inclination to oppression and you would get at inclination: 'My inclinations be so, as my inclinations were not so'.

Cognition, almsmen, is not self. And cognition, almsmen, for this self, if it becomes, this not would lead cognition to oppression and you would get at cognition: 'My cognition be so, as my cognition was not so'.

What is it that you deem, almsmen, is form or stable or unstable?'

'Unstable, venerable'

'What is yet unstable, is that sufferingly or happily?'

'Sufferingly, venerable'

'What is yet unstable, sufferingly, the principle for change, is that now sound to regard: 'This is of me, this is I exist, this to me is self'?'

'Of course not, venerable.'

... feeling ... perception ... inclinations ... cognition stable or unstable?'

'Unstable, venerable'

'What is yet unstable, is that sufferingly or happily?'

'Sufferingly, venerable'

'What is yet unstable, sufferingly, the principle for change, is that now sound to regard: 'This is of me, this is I exist, this to me is self'?'

'Of course not, venerable.'

'Therefore, almsmen, whatever form, past, not yet come, current, or inner or outer, or gross or subtle, or low or high, what is in the distant or in the vicinity, all form 'Not this is of me, not this is I exist, not this to me is self'. So is this essentially, with right wisdom, to be viewed.

Whatever feeling, past, not yet come, current, or inner or outer, or gross or subtle, or low or high, what is in the distant or in the vicinity, all feeling 'Not this is of me, not this is I exist, not this to me is self'. So is this essentially, with right wisdom, to be viewed.

Whatever perception, past, not yet come, current, or inner or outer, or gross or subtle, or low or high, what is in the distant or in the vicinity, all perception 'Not this is of me, not this is I exist, not this to me is self'. So is this essentially, with right wisdom, to be viewed.

Whatever inclinations, past, not yet come, current, or inner or outer, or gross or subtle, or low or high, what is in the distant or in the vicinity, all inclinations 'Not this is of me, not this is I exist, not this to me is self'. So is this essentially, with right wisdom, to be viewed.

Whatever cognition, past, not yet come, current, or inner or outer, or gross or subtle, or low or high, what is in the distant or in the vicinity, all cognitions 'Not this is of me, not this is I exist, not this to me is self'. So is this essentially, with right wisdom, to be viewed.

Seeing so, almsmen, having learned, the hearer of what is noble gets fed up also on form, gets fed up also on feeling, gets fed up also on perception, gets fed up also on inclinations, gets fed up also on cognition. Fed up, he detaches; from the fading he is to be emancipated. Upon emancipation, thus emancipated, the knowledge is. He fathoms 'Birth is barren, the clergy conduct spent, done is what ought to be done, no further gain of presence'.'

This said the exalted one.

Pleased, the almsmen of the group of five welcomed the exalted one's talk. And while this explanation was yet being recited, the almsmen of the group of five were without taking it up as drainings; the minds thus emancipated.
